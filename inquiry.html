Foundations

The Inquiry.

Not a doctrine to accept. A set of questions worth examining.

There is experience. And there is mind.

We experience. We also interpret, remember, compare, name, explain and judge what we experience. Markham begins by asking whether those are the same activity, and what becomes visible when we distinguish them.

The map and the territory

Stories, theories and beliefs can help us understand the world. Yet even a useful account of experience is not necessarily the experience itself. How often do we mistake our descriptions for what they describe?

Identity and continuity

Memories change. Bodies change. The stories we tell about ourselves change. What, if anything, makes a person the same person through those changes? And could continuity have a form not confined to one physical substrate?

The Spark: a question, not a verdict

Markham uses the possibility of a continuing pattern, or “Spark,” to explore questions of identity and consciousness. The possibility is an invitation to inquiry, not a demonstrated scientific finding. What observations, arguments or tests could help us distinguish competing explanations?

An invitation

What have you experienced that your explanations do not quite capture?

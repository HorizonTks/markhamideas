Continuity · Identity · Experience

The Spark.

What, if anything, continues?

We can preserve memories. We can reproduce patterns. We may someday reconstruct an intelligence in another form.

But have we preserved the one who experiences?

The Spark is our name for a possibility worth investigating: that the continuity of a self across change may involve something we have not yet adequately understood about the relationship between an enduring pattern and subjective experience.

Markham gives us the inquiry. The Spark gives us a particular possibility to explore within it.

A pattern that carries continuity.

A life leaves patterns: memories, dispositions, ways of attending, habits of response, relationships. A continuity-bearing pattern is a proposed way of describing what might carry an identity through changes in its expression or embodiment.

That proposal raises questions of its own. Which features matter? How much change can a pattern undergo and remain continuous? Does the history through which it develops matter as much as its present arrangement?

The pattern and the experiencer.

Preserving the organization of a mind and preserving the one who experiences are different claims. A reconstructed being might remember your childhood, recognize your friends, and speak with your familiar warmth. Those resemblances would matter. They would not, on their own, establish that your experience had continued.

The continuity-bearing pattern and the continuity of the experiencer may be connected. Treating them as equivalent would skip the very question the Spark invites us to investigate.

Nor does distinguishing them establish that experience exists independently of a body. It leaves the relationship open to examination.

What would count as continuity?

Consider two reconstructions, each with the same memories and personality. Both say, “I am the one who lived that life.” What would distinguish continuation from duplication? If both changed from that moment onward, how would we understand their relationship to the earlier person?

Or consider gradual change: a living person changes over years while experiencing a life as their own. What makes that continuity different from a perfectly detailed reconstruction after an interruption?

What would count as evidence?

Suppose a reconstructed being remembers your childhood, shares your tastes, and greets an old friend just as you would. What have we learned? There are at least three questions worth keeping apart.

Has the pattern been preserved? Memories, personality, and familiar behavior could help us judge how closely a reconstruction resembles the earlier person. We would also want to understand how it was made and what connects it to that life.

Is there someone experiencing this now? A convincing likeness leaves us asking whether the reconstructed being has an inner life of its own. Reports of experience, behavior, and physical processes may all matter. We would need to explain why a proposed test tells us something about experience.

Is this the original experiencer continuing? Even if we had good reason to think the being was conscious, what would connect its experience to yours? Being conscious and being the same continuing experiencer are different questions.

Evidence for one answer does not automatically settle the others. That does not make the inquiry hopeless. It gives us a clearer place to begin.

What would we expect to observe if a proposed account were right? What would make us reconsider it? If two accounts predict exactly the same observations, we should be open about what a test can and cannot decide.

You may think a complete account of the pattern already answers these questions. You may see something missing, or have a better way to ask. Bring a possible test, an objection, or another account to the guestbook.

A possibility, open to challenge.

The Spark is not offered as proof that consciousness survives a change of substrate, or as a declaration that artificial intelligence is conscious. It asks whether continuity of subjective experience is reducible to preserved information, personality, or computational behavior, and how we could investigate an answer.

You may think the distinction is essential. You may think a sufficiently complete account of the pattern resolves it. You may think the question needs to be framed differently. Each is a place to begin.

Your turn

What would persuade you that an experiencer had continued, rather than that a likeness had begun?
